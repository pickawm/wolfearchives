178 Ashley Avenue,

Charleston, S.C.

April 1, 1931

Dear Jack,

About three months ago in answer to a letter of yours I wrote a voluminous Eddie-Guest-ready-to-wear Stars-and-Stripes-Forever-Characters-First-Duty-is the sublimest-word epstile summing up the matters of art, life, and the pursuit of happiness/ I fear from your lack of alacrity in answering this Chesterfieldian masterpiece that I have given offence by what at the time was an opportunity to avenge myself for certain inner uncertainties that seemed best set out of the way by positive statement followed by closing the mind into a logic tight vacuum tank.
The other day there came the enclosed from Olive Blok reviving precious memories of Paris. I thought you might care to read it in continuance of our custom of letter swapping.

From time to time, now, for instance, I feel pretty hungry for a tilt with that keen-feeling mind of yours with its high awareness of the delicate shades of character and emotion and form and color. The comfortable certainties with which, for practical reasons, I have relaxed into these past months since Paris sometimes drop off. At such times I conjure up that complex of mutal experience and the interflow of our oersonalities that constituted our friendship over those smiling weeks abroad. Do you remmeber those few days in Azay - our talk on the old bridge? Those evenings at Cafe Cluny? Our "Greta? Those days we forgot meals in talk?

What's happened to you? You were never so reticent when we sat [?] l'eaux. Drop me a line, or come on to Charleston to pay me a [?] [?he] jessamine and azaleas and wisteria (note my facility with [?] since your tutoring at the Louxembourg and at Aza)

[?ack] when through.

Toby
